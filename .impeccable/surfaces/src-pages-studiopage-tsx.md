# Studio surface brief

Scope: the Studio route (configurator), plus the Landing, Download and History routes that share its world. Visitor mode: Operate for Studio, Download and History; Persuade for Landing.

Audience and job: hobbyist makers covering a real wall, backsplash or floor with printed relief tiles. Task: answer five questions (the wall, the tile size, the thickness, the texture, the filament), understand the fit in one sentence, then download STL or STEP for every unique piece. Constraints: frontend only, localStorage persistence, English, pure heightfield relief.

Memorable moment: the recommended tile size is computed from the wall the maker just measured, so "no cuts" is true by construction; and the filament they pick washes into the bench, so choosing Matcha Green turns the page green.

Unresolved: none blocking.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Tessera is a well-lit workbench, not a drawing office. The app is warm and tactile: the render of the wall is the product and takes most of the screen, and beside it five numbered steps lead the eye down one column to a single green action. It refuses two things equally: the grey form-sidebar configurator, and the drafting-sheet instrument panel this app used to be, whose lettered borders, ink frames and sheet feet asked the maker to read a technical drawing before they could answer "how big is your wall". Every knob that fewer than one maker in twenty touches lives behind one labelled lid that says on its face whether anything behind it has moved.

OWN-WORLD: A dark espresso bar (#2E241B, lettering #F7F1E6 at 13.49:1) anchors the page over a warm off-white ground (#F2EADC). Panels are soft rounded surfaces (#FFFDF8, radii 11/14/22 px) lifted on warm shadows that are brown rather than grey, with one recessed tone (#EFE7D8) for wells, pills and the fit strip, and a single hairline (#E4D8C4). Ink runs #241C14 / #5C5044 / #6B5D4D, the lightest still AA at 12 px on a panel (6.26:1) and on the recessed tone (5.18:1). Colour carries one job each. Green (#35682A) is the primary action, selection and focus, and it is fixed rather than derived from the spool, so a white or yellow filament can never drag the interface under its contrast floor: it carries #FFFDF8 lettering at 6.53:1, and its soft tint (#EDF3E6) holds green text at 5.87:1 for the step numbers and the Recommended badge. Red (#A3321A) means only "cut, or wrong", and is dark enough to be read as text (6.82:1 on a panel) as well as drawn as a stroke. Green #2C6B3A is the exact-fit tick, amber #A6501E the caution. Over that ground the chosen filament tints the interface at 8%, the ceiling at which a pure black spool still leaves the smallest text at 5.25:1 on the wash. The real colour is the content: the relief swatches and the 104 filament chips are the brightest things on screen, shown as a wall of lit samples with no captions between them, at 44 px under a finger. The espresso bar is the one place the accent is illegal: at 2.29:1 on it, everything up there focuses in the bar's own light ink instead. Type is Archivo throughout, sentence case, tabular figures for every measurement; the expanded uppercase lettering of the old world is gone.

STORY: The maker sees their wall rendered, answers five plain questions in order, watches the wall redraw as they go, reads one sentence telling them how many tiles are whole and how many files they will print, and leaves with a zip.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Two columns under the espresso bar, which carries the six-tile wordmark, the design's name edited in place, the Studio and History tabs as a light pill, and undo, redo and the shortcut key. Left, about two thirds: the 3D wall on a lit bench under a warm lamp from the top left, uncaptioned because it needs no naming, carrying only a Whole wall / One tile switch, a pill naming what is on the bench ("Wavy in Matcha Green"), the View slip (measurements, layer lines, light angle) and a reset. Right, a 33 rem panel whose steps scroll against a foot that never leaves. The steps, each numbered in a soft green disc with its current value at the end of its own heading: 1 Your wall (a width and a height, and the line that says any unit works; the named places that used to sit above them are gone, because they were guesses about someone else's wall), 2 Tile size (the recommendation as a full-width card leading with the size and wearing the word Recommended, its consequence line "32 tiles, no cuts", then the familiar sizes and Custom in an even grid), 3 Thickness (three drawn edge profiles), 4 Texture (the relief samples as a dense wall, the chosen one named in the heading), 5 Filament (the chosen spool named with its line and hex, a row of popular colours, and the full picker). Then one closed lid, "Advanced: gaps, edges, layout, pattern detail and printer", carrying either a changed count or the words "All standard". Then how it falls: a small flat diagram of the wall with cut pieces hatched, a "Show the tiling plan" disclosure holding the full plan, and any red notes with the one move that answers each. The foot holds the fit strip washed in the filament (a green tick, the sentence, the weight), the one green "Get my files", and the quiet line beneath it. Below 1100 px the columns stack, the render leads, and the foot pins itself to the bottom of the screen.

FORM: Direction A, Maker workshop, chosen over the drafting-sheet Setting-out Plan it replaces; seed key d6ac697d. Signature interaction: a change to the wall recomputes the recommended tile size and offers it as a one-click nudge rather than silently moving the maker onto it, while the 3D wall re-lays tile by tile in a stagger wave. Raises kept: the render owns most of the screen (console void), tabular numerals everywhere (datamatics), leader-line annotations pinned in 3D (tensegrity). Raises retired with the drafting world: the lettered and numbered sheet border, the 2 px ink frame, the sheet feet and the revision cell, which charged every screen a border of bookkeeping and taught the maker nothing about their wall.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

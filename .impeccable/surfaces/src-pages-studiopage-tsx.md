# Studio surface brief

Scope: the Studio route (configurator), plus the Landing, Download and History routes that share its world. Visitor mode: Operate for Studio, Download and History; Persuade for Landing.

Audience and job: hobbyist makers covering a real wall, backsplash or floor with printed relief tiles. Task: answer seven questions (the wall, the tile size, the thickness, the texture, the color, how the edges finish, and how the tiles go up), understand the fit in one sentence, then download STL or STEP for every unique piece and any printed parts. Constraints: frontend only, localStorage persistence, English, pure heightfield relief.

Memorable moment: the recommended tile size is computed from the wall the maker just measured, so "no cuts" is true by construction; and the color they pick washes into the bench, so choosing Teal turns the page teal.

Unresolved: none blocking.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Tessera is a well-lit workbench, not a drawing office. The app is warm and tactile: the render of the wall is the product and takes most of the screen, and beside it seven numbered steps lead the eye down one column to a single action in the accent drawn from the tile color. It refuses two things equally: the grey form-sidebar configurator, and the drafting-sheet instrument panel this app used to be, whose lettered borders, ink frames and sheet feet asked the maker to read a technical drawing before they could answer "how big is your wall". Every knob that fewer than one maker in twenty touches lives behind one labelled lid that says on its face whether anything behind it has moved.

OWN-WORLD: A dark espresso bar (#2E241B, lettering #F7F1E6 at 13.49:1) anchors the page over a warm off-white ground (#F2EADC). Panels are soft rounded surfaces (#FFFDF8, radii 11/14/22 px) lifted on warm shadows that are brown rather than grey, with one recessed tone (#EFE7D8) for wells, pills and the fit strip, and a single hairline (#E4D8C4). Ink runs #241C14 / #5C5044 / #6B5D4D, the lightest still AA at 12 px on a panel (6.26:1) and on the recessed tone (5.18:1). Colour carries one job each. The accent is the primary action, selection and focus, and it follows the tile color without letting any tile drag the interface under its contrast floor: accentPalette (src/core/accent.ts) keeps the tile's hue and uses the tile color itself when it already reaches 6.5:1 on a panel, otherwise the lightest color of that hue that does, so a white tile gives a warm grey (#5E5C57) and a yellow one a deep ochre (#765800). It always carries #FFFDF8 lettering at 6.5:1 or better, its hover and press are darker steps of the same hue, and its soft tint keeps accent text and the lightest ink at 4.5:1 or better for the step numbers and selected fills. The default Green tile gives #306918 (6.54:1) over a soft #EAF4E7. src/app/useAccentTheme.ts writes it on the root element with no transition, from the design's color everywhere, except that the home page's sample board leads it while shown. Red (#A3321A) means only "cut, or wrong", and is dark enough to be read as text (6.82:1 on a panel) as well as drawn as a stroke. A fixed green #2C6B3A stays the exact-fit tick, whatever the tile color, and amber #A6501E the caution. Over that ground the chosen tile color tints the interface at 8%, the ceiling at which a pure black tile still leaves the smallest text at 5.25:1 on the wash. The real colour is the content: the relief swatches and the 11 color swatches are the brightest things on screen, shown as a wall of lit samples with no captions between them, at 44 px under a finger. The espresso bar is the one place the accent is illegal: at 2.3:1 at most on it, whatever the tile color, everything up there focuses in the bar's own light ink instead. Type is Archivo throughout, sentence case, tabular figures for every measurement; the expanded uppercase lettering of the old world is gone.

STORY: The maker sees their wall rendered, answers seven plain questions in order, watches the wall redraw as they go, reads one sentence telling them how many tiles are whole and how many files they will print, and leaves with a zip.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Two columns under the espresso bar, which carries the six-tile wordmark, the design's name edited in place, the Studio and History tabs as a light pill, and undo, redo and the shortcut key. Left, about two thirds: the 3D wall on a lit bench under a warm lamp from the top left, uncaptioned because it needs no naming, carrying only a Whole wall / One tile switch, a pill naming what is on the bench ("Wavy in Green", or "Wavy in #12AB34" for a custom color), the View slip (measurements, layer lines, light angle) and a reset, plus, in One tile while the tile shown really has pockets in its back, a Front / Back switch that turns it over to show them, with the printed keys and clips seated in them in a neutral (ink on a light tile, the panel color on a dark one, never the accent). Step 7 may borrow the view for a peek at that back without touching the maker's own view setting; while it does, a pill in the bottom-left corner names what is shown ("Back of tile A", "Show the front") and hands the view back, as does any view control. Right, a 33 rem panel whose steps scroll against a foot that never leaves. Its first line is the step index, pinned to the top of the column while the steps scroll under it (and to the top of the screen below 1100 px): the seven step numbers in a row and a plan mark after a hairline, the step being read (the last heading past a line near the top of what is visible) spelled out in full on the soft accent with its number filled, the others named on hover and to assistive tech, because eight names measured 579 px against the widest rail's 494. A press scrolls only the steps column, lands the step flush under the index and hands its heading the keyboard. The steps, each numbered in a soft accent disc with its current value at the end of its own heading: 1 Your wall (a width and a height, and the line that says any unit works; the named places that used to sit above them are gone, because they were guesses about someone else's wall), 2 Tile size (the recommendation as a full-width card leading with the size and wearing the word Recommended, its consequence line "32 tiles, no cuts", then the familiar sizes and Custom in an even grid), 3 Thickness (three drawn edge profiles; while keys or wall clips are chosen the Light card is disabled and says why), 4 Texture (the relief samples as a dense wall, the chosen one named in the heading), 5 Color (the chosen color named as a preset or "Custom" with its hex, the 11 preset swatches as one radio group, and a "Custom color" cell after them that opens a popover with an HSV wheel, a brightness slider and a hex field; no materials, lines or finishes), 6 Edges (a look only: its heading ends with it, such as "Chamfer joints" or "Rounded edge · chamfer joints"; the edge between tiles LEADS, because every wall has one: Square, Chamfer, Round and Pillow with the edge size under them, moved out of Advanced, and a bevel dragged to nothing ticks Square, because that is what prints. Under it one closed lid, "Border around the wall", whose badge names the profile or says None: most designs never carry a border, and six always-open cards spent the largest half of the step on the answer fewest makers change. Behind the lid the six profiles as a stacked list, each with its drawn cross-section at a size the last few millimetres can actually be told apart at (None, Flat margin, Chamfer, Rounded, Ogee, Raised frame) and, once one is picked, a four-side picker drawn round a small wall with the file count beside it, width and drop sliders, how the pattern meets the edge (Cut, Valleys or Peaks for the three that may cut, Valleys or Peaks for the others) with the fade when the pattern flattens first, and a hint when a cut's drop stops above the valleys), 7 Putting it up (its heading names only what the plans place: "Glued", "Glued · with keys", "On clips", "On clips · with keys"; two questions answered with drawn cards whose accessible name is the card's name alone, "On the wall", Glue or tape ("Adhesive behind") or Wall clips ("Only tape behind"), each an 80 by 56 section drawing, and "Tile to tile", Side by side or Keys, each a back view of two tiles, a chosen card inking its drawn part in ink so it never merges with the accent; then "What changes", a recessed well that is always there, glued and side by side included, whose rows are read off the real plans by one pure function. Each row is a marked eyebrow over its words, not a label in a column: at a 526 px rail a label column left the words about 45 characters a line. What a maker has to act on is flagged at the TOP of the well, before the rows: a choice whose plans placed nothing, and anything this step changed in another one (a thin plate moved to Standard in the same undo step, which undo takes away with the move). Then the rows: "On your tiles", tile A drawn from the back at scale beside its caption, with its slots and pockets and a magnified detail of a part seated in one, and a "See the back of tile A" button that turns the 3D view over, drawn ONLY when something is really cut into that back, because a flat back drew an empty grey rectangle that carried nothing the sentence beside it did not already say; "You'll print", with the real counts, spares and fit test; "You'll need", bought things named generically. The way up and the trade-offs are both read once, so they fold behind one lid inside the well, "How it goes up", badged with the number of steps: "You'll do", the guide's own summary, never reworded, and "Good to know", two or three trade-offs in the design's numbers. Last, "Fit", a control and not a reading: its label and the Snug / Standard / Loose picker share one line, over the sentence that says what the chosen fit means and a secondary "Run the fit test" link to the fit test's own page, only while keys or clips really print. On a wide screen a Keys or Wall clips choice also turns the 3D view to the back of tile A). Then one closed lid, "Advanced: gaps, layout, pattern detail and printer", carrying either a changed count or the words "All standard". Then how it falls: 'Tiling plan', naming the edges that take cuts, over one flat drawing of the wall with cut pieces hatched, a letter on every piece and the point to start from (with clips on, each clip a small outlined bar in its tile, where each tile carries its own, and the start marker reading SO over a legend that says what it is; with keys on, each key a small solid dog-bone across its joint); any red notes with the one move that answers each; and a last lid, 'Your pieces', holding only the table of pieces (then, under "Besides the tiles", the printed parts when keys or clips are placed), whose rows are not clickable (the row of the piece chosen on the drawing is tinted). The foot holds the fit strip washed in the tile color (a green tick, the sentence, which counts tile files and part files apart, the weight), the one accent "Get my files", and the quiet line beneath it. Below 1100 px the columns stack, the render leads, and the foot pins itself to the bottom of the screen.

FORM: Direction A, Maker workshop, chosen over the drafting-sheet Setting-out Plan it replaces; seed key d6ac697d. Signature interaction: while the maker is on Recommended, a change to the wall, joint, layout or printer carries the tile to the new recommendation within the same undo step; a size they picked or typed is never rewritten, and the new recommendation is offered beside it as a one-click nudge. Meanwhile the 3D wall re-lays tile by tile in a stagger wave. Raises kept: the render owns most of the screen (console void), tabular numerals everywhere (datamatics), leader-line annotations pinned in 3D (tensegrity). Raises retired with the drafting world: the lettered and numbered sheet border, the 2 px ink frame, the sheet feet and the revision cell, which charged every screen a border of bookkeeping and taught the maker nothing about their wall.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
